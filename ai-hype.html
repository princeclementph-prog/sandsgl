AI Hype

Buyer Beware: Do Not Believe the Hype

AI is transforming work, but it’s not magic. It’s a powerful tool with real limits. Here’s the straight talk on what it can and can’t do — and why human judgment is still essential.

AI Hallucinations

One of the most dangerous traits of current AI systems is their tendency to hallucinate. AI doesn't truly "know" facts — it predicts what words are most likely to come next. This often results in it confidently inventing information, citing nonexistent sources, or creating plausible-sounding but completely false answers.

AI Memory Limits

AI has a limited context window. It can only remember a certain amount of information from the current conversation. In long discussions, it quickly forgets earlier details. It also has no persistent memory between separate sessions unless specifically engineered with external tools.

Its Work Must Be Reviewed

Because of hallucinations and memory constraints, every output from AI should be carefully reviewed by a human. Never accept AI-generated content at face value — especially for important decisions, legal work, medical advice, or client communications. Human judgment, verification, and accountability remain essential.

AI: A Smart Tool — With Real Boundaries

AI changes how we work — like spreadsheets or email — boosting speed, ideas, and decisions. But it’s not magic. Hype says it’ll do everything; reality says it helps… with limits.

It forgets stuff quickly. Long conversations? Details slip away like notes in a busy meeting.

It sometimes invents answers. If info is missing, it’ll fill gaps wrong — always verify important facts.

It handles one focus at a time. Great for quick tasks, but juggling too much? Accuracy drops fast.

It copies patterns, not real thinking. Mimics what it’s seen, but misses nuance or judgment — humans still call the shots.

It needs good input to shine. Garbage data in? Garbage out. Clean your basics first.

It won’t replace your team. Boosts efficiency, but big calls need people — AI is an assistant, not a boss.
