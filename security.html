Security Content Automation Protocol (SCAP)

Security Content Automation Protocol is a standardized framework developed to automate the assessment and management of security vulnerabilities and compliance requirements across computer systems.

It enables organizations to perform consistent automated checks that align with regulatory mandates and reduce manual errors in security operations.

The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)

The National Institute of Standards and Technology is a United States government agency that created and maintains Security Content Automation Protocol.

NIST publishes the standards and reference implementations used by organizations worldwide.

Potential Legal and Compliance Risks

Failure to adopt SCAP can result in regulatory violations, contractual compliance failures, audit failures, legal penalties, and increased exposure to data breach lawsuits.

Explanation of CVE

A Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures (CVE) entry is a unique identifier assigned to publicly known cybersecurity weaknesses in software or hardware.

Example: CVE-2017-0144 (EternalBlue)

EternalBlue is a vulnerability in Microsoft's SMBv1 protocol that allows remote attackers to execute arbitrary code on unpatched systems.

It became globally known during the WannaCry ransomware attack in 2017.

During a SCAP-compliant scan, tools automatically inspect systems for conditions matching known vulnerabilities and provide remediation guidance.
